# Novice playtest protocol — no results collected yet

Target:3–5 willing volunteers with no prior exposure to this game. This is a small usability observation, not a controlled educational study. Do not invent users, quotes or scores. Obtain consent before any recording; no names are needed.

## Before play (one minute)
Say: "This is a short puzzle game about choosing a test instrument. Please think aloud. I am testing the game, not you. You can stop whenever you want."
Ask: "Have you studied quantum computing before?" Record a coarse answer, no personal information.
Ask them to predict whether testing a device once can always distinguish four different faults. Do not teach the answer yet.

## During play (five minutes)
Let them follow the opening, try the resettable training cable, and attempt a real repair without coaching, then choose a chapter. Do not explain the probe names for them: the walkthrough is what is being tested. Record:
- Time until first successful action.
- Whether the next action is obvious without opening Help.
- Whether they understand the signal preview versus a confirmed repair and the one-use rule.
- Whether they can explain what a repair switch changes and voluntarily continue after the first device.
- Which instrument they select forbit-only, phase-only, andfour-fault jobs.
- Where they hesitate, misread bits, open help or abandon.
- Any rendering, navigation or explanation problem.

Only intervene if completely stuck; record the intervention, rather than later calling the run unassisted.

## After play (two minutes)
Ask open questions BEFORE revealing solutions:
1. "What does a Bit probe tell you? What can it miss?"
2. "What does the Phase probe do differently? Why did the first test miss that fault?"
3. "Why is the linked probe not just testing the customer's cable twice?"
4. "When would you save an entangled reference?"
5. "What changes between an ordinary probe and the paired probe?"
6. "Could the spare qubit tell you the fault without getting the probe back?"
7. "Were these real hardware results or a simulation?"
8. "What was confusing or boring, and would you choose another chapter?"
9. "What did you think would happen when you moved the switches?"
