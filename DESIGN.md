# Art direction and design system

## Current direction
The user liked the cream ticket and quirky drawings but rejected the wordy, quiz-like interaction. The playable science apprenticeship is now implemented, but the user still reports not understanding it; do not call novice comprehension solved. The previous front page is explicitly rejected: the entrance must be a full-viewport cinematic forest and forward camera journey, not a website headline beside an illustration. Current entrance scope: docs/CINEMATIC-ENTRANCE-CONTRACT.md. Preserve the workshop/tutorial/repair contracts in docs/SCIENCE-WALKTHROUGH-CONTRACT.md and docs/REDESIGN-CONTRACT.md.

## Thesis
Arrive through woods and fog, learn what a probe does by operating a resettable cable, then test a customer's cable once and apply a repair. Whimsy lives in the workshop, taped ticket, honest ink stamp and ghost, not in slogans. Teach one thing through an action and a visible result; do not turn the expanded tutorial into an instruction sheet.

## Authoritative tokens
Use web/src/tokens.css: background --ink #151b18, surface #202b24, elevated #2b382e, paper/foreground #eee6cf, paper-ink #28362a, muted #b7c2ad, mint #bfe3a4, amber #f5bb70, error #ffaaa0, border #637461 and border-soft #39463a. Display Georgia serif; body native/system sans; monospace only for short signal/readout values. No remote fonts or competing palette. Preserve measured contrast; do not make subdued text unreadable to reduce visual weight.

## Composition
- Opening: a full-viewport layered forest, game-title overlay and immediately usable Open the shop. A forward camera push through the woods leads to the keeper. No website header/footer, framed illustration, split hero or premise paragraph. Chapters and small Help/Menu controls behave like title-screen options; Skip/Escape and reduced motion bypass the journey. This exception does not remove the useful workshop header inside the actual game.
- Story opening: a brief encounter with the keeper, Learn the tools and Skip to repairs. Training teaches the tools on resettable known cables before the first customer query; replay remains available through Help/Menu. No customer query is triggered by watching, practising or skipping.
- Tutorial: one concept, visible apparatus, measured output and one primary action per stage. Bit, phase and the linked reference are introduced through concrete contrasts; no unexplained instrument choice. See docs/SCIENCE-WALKTHROUGH-CONTRACT.md for exact science and API boundaries.
- Bench: compact cream ticket at left; larger device/signal and current-step controls at right. Keep the one-query boundary and remaining linked probes understandable, but avoid repeated caveats. Do not repeat the ticket's story as a second paragraph elsewhere.
- Test: compact Bit / Phase / Linked probe choices and a single Test cable action. Guided training may recommend an experiment; customer jobs explain only the selected instrument's capability and cost, leaving the probe choice to the player.
- Repair: the measured signal remains beside bit/phase switches. Customer switches trigger a short visible trace pulse without changing the recorded evidence or evaluating the proposed correction. Guided training retains its labeled correction preview. Apply repair commits the customer decision. No multiple-choice fault cards, diagnosis quiz heading or answer-letter grid.
- Resolution: device visibly works or remains broken based on the actual server result. One short outcome line and a continue action. Circuit/why-details are optional.
- Chapters: actual short Bit, Phase and Entanglement routes, not cosmetic section labels. Continuing the original story preserves its server session; choosing a different route starts a new one and requires clear confirmation when abandoning work.
- Inside-game header: brand, Help, Menu. Put science, sound, chapters and reset in Menu. Footer only discloses Qiskit simulation. Retain evidence/report features without advertising all of them during a repair. The title-screen cinema hides this website-like chrome and overlays only small game-option controls on its world.

## Responsive and interaction rules
Mobile order: short ticket, meaningful signal, controls; avoid giant repeated bench artwork below the buttons. Aim for the primary action within an ordinary phone-sized first view; do not fake this by clipping essential content. Validate 390 and 320 CSS pixels as well as intermediate/desktop sizes. Controls target at least 44 CSS pixels, visible amber focus, native semantics and keyboard activation. Use role=switch and aria-checked for repair toggles; labels do not change to mysterious symbols when selected.

The large visual must reflect real state: unknown before a test, measured afterward, and confirmed only on server resolution. A customer trace may pulse cosmetically while editing repairs, but the measured bits and settled trace stay fixed. Only guided training uses a labeled speculative correction preview. A probe that did not measure phase cannot reveal phase through a visual preview. Bell display order remains c1c0. Motion communicates a test or repair, never substitutes for an actual response; reduced motion preserves all information. No flashing or time pressure. Sound stays opt-in. A Menu motion control provides a pause alternative; reduced-motion users see the fully revealed house and static stamps immediately. The CRT's background hum/static and short switch/stamp cues are quiet procedural effects, not measurement results. Ticket/stamp animation keys off real transitions and does not restart on every selection or switch render.

Keep uncertainty/error/retry states, captured revisions, safe 409 reconciliation, saved-shift resume and modal focus restoration. Detailed proof, probability tables and limitations remain available in optional science/circuit views, not repeated on the main bench. Automated checks establish function/readability, not fun; the next user playtest is the actual validation of the redesigned experience.
